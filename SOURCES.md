# Sources - Tuesday, 24 November 2026

Every story on the page was carried by at least two distinct outlets
in the day's cross-feed research before it was selected. The numbers
quoted in the "By the Numbers" strip are taken from the stories
themselves, never rounded or combined. `sources.json` carries the
same record in machine-readable form, including a short context

## Feature stories

1. **US to blacklist Asian AI firm Megaspeed after Nvidia scandal**
   - Carried by: Al Jazeera, BBC News, The Guardian.
   - The story: Washington moved to add Megaspeed to its export
     control list. The firm stands accused of funneling advanced AI
     chips to China, and the case widened when a former executive's
     affair with Nvidia chief Jensen Huang became public.
   - Why it leads: it ties export controls, the AI chip race and a
     corporate scandal into the day's most consequential business
     story, and three major outlets carried it independently.

2. **Three killed and dozens missing after fire at Indonesian factory**
   - Carried by: BBC News, DW, The Guardian.
   - The story: a fire tore through a confectionery factory, killing
     at least three people, with many more feared dead and dozens of
     workers still missing as the search went on.
   - Why it is a feature: a mass-casualty industrial disaster with
     the toll still unresolved at press time.

3. **Federal court clears way for army to detonate bomb-making material in Iowa**
   - Carried by: AP News, The Guardian.
   - The story: a federal appeals court cleared the US Army to
     detonate several tons of degraded bomb-making material at an
     Iowa ammunition plant. Hours earlier Donald Trump had halted
     the planned demolition and asked the court to sign off on what
     he called the safest way to resolve the situation first, making
     his go-ahead subject to the incoming governor-elect as well.
   - Why it is a feature: an unusual public reversal followed by a
     court ruling, on a story with real local stakes.

## What we did not print

For each story, the fact that stayed off the page and why. The
page has no body text, so every story leaves almost everything
out; these are the omissions a reader is most likely to wonder
about.

- **Megaspeed:** the mechanics of the alleged funneling. The
  accusation is printed; the routing detail lives in the linked
  reporting, because a one-image page cannot carry it honestly.
- **Factory fire:** the factory's name and owner. The wires led
  with the toll and the missing, and the page follows the wires.
- **Iowa:** the tonnage is described as "several tons" because
  that is the figure the carriers agreed on; a more precise
  number appeared in single-outlet copy and was not printed.
- **Merkury:** the drone type and launch point. CBS and Reuters
  carried the sinking and the damage; the technical detail was
  not in both, so it is not on the page.
- **Libya reaction:** Libya's own condemnation is in the context
  record but not the headline, which belongs to the two principals
  the wire pair named.
- **COP31:** the country-by-country positions. An analysis story
  summarises blocs; the page prints the summary, not the roll
  call.
- **Gaza:** the report's methodology. The 99% is printed with its
  attribution (Oxfam says) precisely because the page cannot show
  the working.

## Headline fidelity

Headlines were taken from the carrying outlets as published. The
one editorial liberty in this edition is punctuation: the Oxfam
headline's inner quotation marks are single quotes in the page's
serif, matching the outlets' own rendering. No headline was
rewritten to sharpen it, and no number in a headline or in the
strip was rounded: the day offered exactly one hard figure, 99%,
and it is printed as reported.

## Selection notes

The seven stories are what the two-outlet rule produced from the
day's feeds, not a balanced ticket assembled by hand. Three
consequences are worth recording:

- There is no sports story and no markets story on the page,
  because none in the day's feeds was carried by two of the
  tracked outlets in a form that fit the format. The rule, not a
  preference, kept them off.
- Two of the seven stories are the same event (the Libya strike)
  from different wire pairs. The rule counts carriers per story,
  and each telling independently passed it; merging them would
  have produced a stronger single story but a less honest record
  of how the news actually arrived.
- The Gaza story is an assessment published on the day, not an
  event of the day. It was selected because two BBC brands carried
  it as a lead item and because its figure, 99%, was the day's
  most checkable number. Its presence is also why the numbers
  strip leads with a figure from the news instead of a count.
