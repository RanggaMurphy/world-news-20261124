# Export controls, in brief

Background for feature 1 (the Megaspeed blacklist). This file
explains the instrument; the dossier (stories/01-megaspeed.md)
records the case.

## What an export-control list is

A government register of foreign companies that may not receive
specified technology without a licence. Listing is an
administrative act, not a criminal verdict: it does not require a
trial, and it takes effect on publication. For the listed firm
the practical consequence is immediate: suppliers of the
controlled goods must stop, ask, or risk their own penalties.

## Why AI chips are controlled

Advanced accelerators are dual-use in the strictest sense: the
same hardware trains a translation model and a targeting model.
Controls therefore attach to the chip's capability, not to its
buyer's stated purpose, which is why enforcement focuses on
diversion: hardware sold to an permitted buyer and quietly
re-routed to a prohibited one. That diversion pattern is exactly
what Megaspeed stands accused of.

## What "funneling" means in the record

The carriers' word for the allegation is funneling: chips moving
through an intermediary so that the paper trail ends somewhere
permitted while the hardware ends somewhere else. The page prints
the accusation and the listing; the routing detail, which would
prove or disprove the funnel, is in the linked reporting and in
the listing documents themselves when they publish.

## Why the scandal mattered to the story

Export-control cases are usually paperwork. This one became a
scandal because a former executive's affair with Nvidia's chief
executive became public, attaching the industry's most visible
name to the file. The desk's judgement, recorded in the dossier,
is that the affair explains the story's reach, not its
importance: the listing would matter without it.

## What would change the story

- The formal listing document, if it names conduct beyond what
  the carriers reported.
- A statement from Nvidia addressing the funneling claim itself.
- Any licence grants after listing, which would soften the act
  from a wall into a gate.
