# Naval drones and the widening map

Background for grid stories 1 and 2 (the Merkury sinking and the
Libya reaction). Dossiers: stories/04-merkury-sinking.md and
stories/05-libya-reaction.md.

## The campaign this strike extends

Ukraine's naval drones have been the asymmetric answer to a
larger fleet: uncrewed surface vessels, cheap enough to lose,
that have pushed Russia's Black Sea Fleet away from Ukraine's
coast. Every confirmed success of that campaign until this
edition happened in or near the Black Sea. The Merkury sinking
happened off Libya, in the Mediterranean, which is a different
claim: not that the drones work, but that they travel.

## Why a sinking in a third country's port matters

A strike in a Libyan port involves a state that is not a party
to the war, which is why the reaction story exists: Libya's
UN-backed government condemned the strike on its territory, and
the Kremlin framed the operation as escalation. A sinking at sea
is a military fact; a sinking in someone's harbour is also a
diplomatic one, and the page's top grid row is built to show
both halves.

## The sanctioned cargo ship

The damaged second vessel matters because of its status, not its
size: a sanctioned ship is part of the shadow logistics that move
restricted cargo, and damaging one signals that the campaign's
target set includes the supply system, not only warships.
Reuters anchored that status, and the headline prints it on
Reuters' authority.

## How to read the two headlines together

Left cell: what happened, in the fullest wording the grid could
hold. Right cell: what the principals said it meant, attributed.
Read in that order, the row is a complete news item. Read in
reverse, it is a claim looking for its event, which is why the
order is fixed by the design and recorded in EDITION.md.
