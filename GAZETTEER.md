# Gazetteer - places named in the 24 November 2026 edition

Short notes on the places the headlines name, and why each one is
where the story happened. Regions are also recorded per story in
`sources.json`.

## Iowa, United States

The ammunition plant at the centre of feature 3 holds several
tons of degraded bomb-making material. The legal fight was over
how to dispose of it: detonation in place, which the Army favoured
and the appeals court ultimately cleared, after a presidential
pause that made the ruling a national story.

## Indonesia

The confectionery factory fire (feature 2) is the edition's
deadliest single event: three confirmed deaths at press time and
dozens missing, the kind of industrial fire whose toll rises as
the building is searched.

## Libya

Two of the four world-grid stories happen off the Libyan coast,
where Ukraine's naval drones sank the Russian warship Merkury in
port and damaged a sanctioned cargo ship. A Ukrainian strike in a
Libyan port is precisely what made the story world news: the
war's naval front reached the Mediterranean.

## Gaza

The Oxfam report (grid story 7) assesses the whole territory:
signs of possible contamination across almost all of it, which is
where the strip's 99% comes from. The headline's phrase "toxic
wasteland" is Oxfam's own.

## China (named in the story, not the dateline)

Megaspeed's alleged chip funneling was aimed at China; the
blacklist is a US decision about an Asian firm, but China is the
destination that makes it a front-page story.

## The climate talks (no single place)

The COP31 analysis is the edition's one placeless story: its
subject is the failure of a negotiation, distributed across oil
producers, a boycotting US and a finance dispute. It runs in the
grid with a photograph rather than a panel because the talks
themselves were photographed even though the story is an
argument.

## How places map to the page

- Features: an export-control decision (US/Asia), a disaster
  (Indonesia), a court ruling (Iowa).
- Grid: one war, two angles (Libya), one negotiation (global),
  one assessment (Gaza).
- The edition spans four continents without leaving the format:
  place is carried by the headlines and this gazetteer, never by
  body text on the page itself.

## The Mediterranean and the Black Sea

The Libya stories matter geographically because of where they did
not happen: not in the Black Sea, where Ukraine's naval drone
campaign has mostly operated, but in the Mediterranean, off a
North African coast. A drone strike sinking a warship in a Libyan
port extends the war's map by a whole sea, which is why both wire
pairs treated the location as part of the news, and why the
gazetteer gives Libya two of its entries' worth of attention
across the pair of stories.

## Benghazi

The port named in the coverage of the strike is Benghazi, Libya's
second city and its main eastern harbour. The condemnation quoted
in the coverage came from Libya's UN-backed government, for whom
a foreign strike in a Libyan port is a sovereignty story as much
as a war story. The page's panel label for the sinking story says
UKRAINE, because the actor was Ukrainian; the place, recorded
here, is Benghazi.

## Jakarta and the factory belt

The Guardian's dating puts the factory fire on 22 November, and
its URL names Jakarta: the confectionery plant sat in the capital
region's industrial belt, where large food factories employ the
kind of workforce that makes "dozens missing" a plausible and
terrible phrase. The gazetteer records the place as Indonesia on
the page's data because the wires' headlines said Indonesia; the
Jakarta detail lives here, where the record is fuller.

## Washington

Two of the three features were decided in Washington in the
broad sense: the export control list is a Commerce Department
instrument, and the Iowa pause was a presidential post before it
was a court ruling. Washington never appears in a headline on the
page, yet it is the edition's most powerful place: the city whose
decisions about chips and explosives bookend the features. The
gazetteer names it so the map of the day is honest about where
its power sat.

## Kyiv and Moscow

The reaction story is a two-capital story. Kyiv's part is a
president praising a drone operation by name; Moscow's part is a
warning phrased generally, about a trend rather than a single
strike, with a refusal to comment on the specific attacks. The
gazetteer records both capitals because the story's geography is
diplomatic, not physical: nothing happened in either city, and
everything about the story's meaning did. That is also why the
story sits in the grid rather than the features: its facts are
statements, and statements get half weight on this page unless
two wires treat them as the event itself.

## The factory's district

The fire coverage names Indonesia and, in the Guardian's dating,
Jakarta. Industrial fires of this kind are a district story
before they are a national one: the plant, its workforce and its
shift pattern decide the toll. The record available on the day
did not name the district in two-outlet copy, so the gazetteer
keeps the place at the level the wires agreed on, and this entry
exists to say that the narrower geography is known to matter and
was deliberately not guessed at.

## Datelines

The gazetteer's discipline, stated once at the end: places are
named no more precisely than the two-outlet record allows.
Cities appear only where the wires put them (Jakarta inside a
URL, Benghazi inside the copy); otherwise regions carry the
story, and Washington appears for what it decided rather than
where a reporter stood. Finer precision would pretend to a
knowledge this edition never had.

## Three more places the record touches

- **The Mediterranean** is an actor in this edition, not a
  backdrop: it is the sea Ukraine's campaign reached, the sea
  Libya's coast fronts, and the reason the sinking outranks a
  Black Sea strike of the same size. The gazetteer treats seas
  as places when the news does.
- **The Eighth Circuit** is a place in the institutional sense:
  a federal appeals court whose district includes Iowa, and the
  body whose clearance is feature 3's fact. Institutional places
  get entries when a story's outcome depends on which one acted.
- **The export-control list** is a place in the legal sense: a
  register a company is placed on, with consequences that follow
  it everywhere it trades. Megaspeed's story is about being moved
  to that place, and the headline's verb (blacklist) is the
  register's everyday name.
