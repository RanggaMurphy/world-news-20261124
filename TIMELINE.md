# Timeline - how the day's stories unfolded

The front page freezes the day at press time. This note puts the
seven stories back into the order their events actually happened,
which is not the order they sit on the page: the page leads with
consequence, the timeline with chronology.

## Before the edition day

- The Megaspeed affair had been building for days: accusations
  that the firm funneled AI chips to China, and the revelation of
  a former executive's affair with Jensen Huang, set up the
  blacklist decision that landed as this edition was assembled.
- The Indonesian factory fire began on 22 November (Guardian
  dating); by press time the confirmed toll was three dead with
  dozens still missing.
- Gaza's contamination, per Oxfam, is cumulative: the report
  describes damage from the whole campaign, published as a single
  assessment rather than a response to one strike.

## Monday 23 November

- The Guardian and BBC dated their Megaspeed and Iowa stories to
  23 November: the appeals court process on the Iowa detonation
  ran through the day, with Trump first halting the demolition and
  asking the court to sign off, and the Eighth Circuit then
  clearing the way.
- Ukraine's strike on Russian shipping off Libya was reported
  through 23 November: the Merkury sinking, the cargo ship damage,
  Zelensky's praise and the Kremlin's warning all belong to the
  same news cycle.
- The COP31 talks collapsed into their post-mortem: the BBC's
  divided-world analysis is dated to the talks' failure, with oil
  producers' interests, the US boycott and the finance dispute
  named as the causes.

## Tuesday 24 November (edition day)

- Al Jazeera's Megaspeed filing is dated 24 November, the third
  independent confirmation that let the story lead the page.
- The Oxfam Gaza report led the BBC's morning coverage, supplying
  the edition's single hard number: 99%.

## State of each story at press time

- **Megaspeed:** decided in principle (the blacklist move was
  reported as Washington's intent), with the formal listing the
  expected next step. The page prints the intent, as reported.
- **Factory fire:** open. Three dead confirmed, dozens missing;
  the toll was expected to move, and the headline prints both
  numbers exactly as they stood.
- **Iowa:** decided. The court's clearance is the end of the
  legal sequence that began with the presidential pause; the
  detonation itself is an operational matter after press time.
- **Libya:** one event, two open threads: the military facts
  (sinking, damage) were confirmed by two wires, while the
  political thread (praise, warning, condemnation) was still
  developing.
- **COP31:** closed as an event, open as an argument; the
  analysis story is the edition's record of the argument's first
  day.
- **Gaza / Oxfam:** an assessment, not an event; its press-time
  state is simply its publication, and its number is printed
  with its attribution attached.


## Reading the page against this timeline

Features 1 and 3 are both, underneath, stories about governments
deciding what may move and what may blow up: export controls on
chips, a court order on explosives. The Libya pair shows one
event splitting into a military story (what sank) and a political
story (who praised, who warned). The timeline keeps those pairs
together even where the page separates them for layout.

## How the wires moved

A note on sequence, because the timeline above orders events, not
reporting. The factory fire was a 22 November story still
developing. The Megaspeed and Iowa stories broke on the wires on
23 November and were still being filed on the morning of the
24th, which is when Al Jazeera's Megaspeed piece supplied the
third confirmation. The Libya operation's two tellings arrived as
a pair: the sinking first as a military report, the reaction
within the same cycle. The COP31 analysis and the Oxfam report
are both morning-of publications, written about longer arcs. The
edition therefore mixes three kinds of time: a disaster still
unfolding, decisions taken the day before, and assessments of
things that took months. The date line claims only the day of
publication, and this section exists so that nobody reads it as
claiming more.

## What would have changed the page

Three developments would have reordered this front page had they
landed before press time: a confirmed higher toll from the
factory fire (it would have led), a formal Megaspeed listing
document (it would have sharpened, not moved, the lead), and a
Russian operational response in the Mediterranean (it would have
merged the two Libya stories into one larger one and freed a
grid slot). None had landed; the watch list in `sources.json`
records them for the next edition.

## Each story's confirmation path

How each story crossed the two-outlet line, in the order the
research recorded it:

- **Megaspeed** arrived with three carriers already attached
  (BBC News, The Guardian, Al Jazeera), the strongest confirmation
  of the day; it was a feature from the first pass.
- **Factory fire** likewise arrived with three (BBC News, DW,
  The Guardian), and its still-open toll kept it a feature
  through every later pass.
- **Iowa** crossed on two (AP News, The Guardian) and earned its
  feature slot on the reversal inside it: pause, then clearance.
- **Merkury sinking** crossed on CBS News and Reuters, a wire
  pair with no overlap in ownership, the cleanest two-outlet
  confirmation in the grid.
- **Libya reaction** crossed on AP News and BBC News; it is the
  only story whose confirmation pair shares no outlet with its
  companion story's pair, which is part of why the pair could be
  printed as two stories.
- **COP31** crossed on the two BBC brands, the weakest
  confirmation of the day by the edition's own counting note;
  it was kept because the analysis was the day's only argument
  piece and both brands carried it as a lead.
- **Gaza / Oxfam** crossed on the same pair, and was kept for
  its number: the report's 99% was the hardest figure available
  to the strip.
