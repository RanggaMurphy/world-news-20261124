# Story 7 - Gaza, the Oxfam report (Around the World 4)

**Headline as printed:** Israeli attacks have turned Gaza into
'toxic wasteland', Oxfam says

**Position:** Around the World, row 2, right cell. The grid's
closing story, placed last so the page ends on evidence rather
than opinion, and the source of the numbers strip's lead figure.

**Carriers:** BBC, BBC News (see OUTLETS.md, "The pair problem").

**Region:** Gaza.

## What happened

An Oxfam report concluded that Israeli attacks have turned Gaza
into a toxic wasteland. The report found signs of possible
contamination across almost all of the territory as a result of
Israel's actions. It is an assessment of cumulative damage, not
a response to a single strike, published as one document and
carried as a lead item by both BBC brands on the edition day.

## The facts on record

- Oxfam reported signs of possible contamination across almost
  all of Gaza.
- The report attributes the contamination to Israel's actions.
- The 99% in the numbers strip is this story's figure.
- The phrase "toxic wasteland" is Oxfam's own wording.

## Why it closes the grid

It carries the day's only hard number, and the strip needed a
figure from the news rather than another count of the paper's
own process. It also balances the page geographically and
morally: the edition's last word is an assessment of what the
wars on the page do to the ground they are fought on.

## How the headline was chosen

The headline keeps Oxfam's phrase in quotation marks and its
attribution at the end, because the claim is an organisation's
finding, not a wire's observation. The page prints the finding
as a finding; a reader who wants the methodology follows the
URLs, and the strip's label repeats the attribution ("Oxfam
says") so the number never travels without its source.

## What was not printed

The report's methodology and its contaminant-by-contaminant
detail: the page cannot show the working, so it shows the
attribution instead, twice. No figure from the report other than
the 99% is quoted anywhere in the edition.

## Watch next

Whether other agencies corroborate the contamination assessment,
and any official response to the 99% figure.
