# Story 2 - Indonesian factory fire (feature 2)

**Headline as printed:** Three killed and dozens missing after
fire at Indonesian factory

**Position:** Feature 2, centre column. The page's centre of
gravity, deliberately: the day's deadliest event sits between
the two American decision stories.

**Carriers:** BBC News, DW, The Guardian (three).

**Region:** Indonesia.

## What happened

A fire tore through a confectionery factory in Indonesia. At
least three people were confirmed killed, and many more were
feared dead: dozens of workers were still missing as rescuers
searched the building. The Guardian's dating puts the fire on
22 November; by press time the toll was still unresolved, which
is why the headline carries both numbers, the confirmed and the
missing, exactly as they stood.

## The facts on record

- The fire hit a confectionery factory in Indonesia.
- At least three people were confirmed killed.
- Dozens of workers were still missing at press time.
- Many more were feared dead as the search continued.

## Why it is a feature

It is the only story on the page where people died that day in
the record, and its central fact is unfinished: a toll that will
move. A front page that led with two policy decisions and buried
its only mass-casualty event in the grid would be misreading its
own day. The centre column is the page's conscience.

## How the headline was chosen

The headline is DW's framing in substance: killed and missing,
in that order, confirmed before feared. No adjective is added.
The word "dozens" is the carriers' word, kept because a more
precise figure was not in two-outlet copy.

## What was not printed

The factory's name and owner stayed off the page because the
wires led with the toll, and the page follows the wires. The
district is not named either: the record supports Jakarta at
the level of a URL, not at the level of two-outlet copy, and the
gazetteer says so rather than guessing.

## Watch next

The missing count: whether the dozens still missing are found,
and the confirmed toll in the next official statement.
