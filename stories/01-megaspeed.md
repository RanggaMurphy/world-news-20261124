# Story 1 - Megaspeed blacklist (feature 1)

**Headline as printed:** US to blacklist Asian AI firm Megaspeed
after Nvidia scandal

**Position:** Feature 1, left column. The edition's lead story.

**Carriers:** Al Jazeera, BBC News, The Guardian (three, the
joint-strongest confirmation of the day).

**Region:** United States / Asia.

## What happened

Washington moved to add the Asian AI firm Megaspeed to its export
control list. The firm stands accused of funneling advanced AI
chips to China, in breach of the controls that are supposed to
keep those chips out of Chinese hands. The case became a public
scandal when a former executive's affair with Jensen Huang, the
chief executive of Nvidia, became public, tying the world's most
valuable chip company to the allegation by association.

## The facts on record

- Megaspeed was accused of funneling advanced AI chips to China.
- The US response was a place on the export control list.
- A former executive's affair with Jensen Huang widened the
  scandal.
- Three outlets carried the story independently on the day.

## Why it leads

Four elements meet in this story and in no other on the page: a
state decision (the blacklist), a strategic industry (AI chips),
a destination that matters geopolitically (China) and a human
scandal that made the file public. It is also the only feature
whose consequences run forward for months: a listing changes
what a whole supply chain may legally do. The page leads with
consequence, and this story has the most of it.

## How the headline was chosen

The headline names the consequence (blacklist) before the cause
(scandal), because a reader scanning the page needs the decision
first. It is the carriers' own wording in substance: Al Jazeera
framed the move as an export-control act, the BBC and the
Guardian led with the blacklist itself.

## What was not printed

The mechanics of the alleged funneling (routing, intermediaries,
quantities) stayed off the page: they were not in two-outlet
copy, and a one-image format cannot carry them honestly. The
affair is named because the carriers named it as the reason the
case widened; no detail of it is printed or linked beyond the
carriers' own articles.

## Watch next

Whether the formal listing names Megaspeed as reported, and
whether Nvidia comments on the funneling claim.
